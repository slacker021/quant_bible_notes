# Primary and Secondary Sources

The Quant's Bible utilizes many references sourced variously, ranging from traditional textbooks to YouTube videos. Instead of rederiving everything from scratch, the use of already-established literature is makes providing relevant knowledge—both by the author and AI agents—much easier and more convenient. Thus, these notes owe their existence to the original writers and publishers of these references. 

There are two types of sources that are used as references: *Primary* and *Secondary* sources: 
1. **Primary Sources:** They are usually in the form of textbooks, serving as the main reference for a single volume. It not only provides the facts presented, but  also follow  the manner of which topics are introduced and presented. Each volume uses a single reference only so as to maintain pedagogical consistency, as using multiple primary sources for a single volume would result in an incoherent pedagogical framework. Additionally, it is difficult and time-costly to have to adapt a piece of information from another source as it has to be standardized to fit these notes' framework. 
2. **Secondary Sources:** Although a primary source provides the framework and topic outline that a volume follows, it is usually incomplete. Whether because it lacks relevant theorems, omits proofs, does not include meaningful examples, or poorly explains concepts, a single source for a volume is woefully inadequate. Thus, *secondary sources* are warranted in providing grounded expansions for pedagogical gaps that may be present in the primary source. A secondary source can be of many formats—textbook, website, YouTube video, published papers, or random lecture notes that are uploaded to the internet by various academic departments from institutions of higher learning and corporate departments from financial firms.
