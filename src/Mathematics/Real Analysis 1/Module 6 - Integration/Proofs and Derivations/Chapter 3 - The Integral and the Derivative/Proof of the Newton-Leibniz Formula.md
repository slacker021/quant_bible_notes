Let $[a, b] \subset \mathbb{R}$ be a non-degenerate compact interval with $a < b$, let $f \in \mathcal{R}[a, b]$ be a Riemann integrable function, and suppose there exists a primitive $\Phi: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying:
1. $\Phi$ is continuous on the closed interval $[a, b]$, that is, $\Phi \in C[a, b]$;
2. $\Phi$ is differentiable on the open interval $(a, b)$ with derivative:
$$
\begin{gather}
\Phi'(x) = f(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in (a, b) \tag{1}
\end{gather}
$$

Let $P$ be an arbitrary partition of the interval $[a, b]$ defined by the ordered set of division nodes:
$$
\begin{gather}
P = \{x_0, x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\} \tag{2}
\end{gather}
$$
satisfying:
$$
\begin{gather}
a = x_0 < x_1 < x_2 < \dots < x_n = b \tag{3}
\end{gather}
$$

For each index $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$, let $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$ denote the $i$-th subinterval of the partition, let the subinterval length be denoted by:
$$
\begin{gather}
\Delta x_i := x_i - x_{i-1} > 0 \tag{4}
\end{gather}
$$
and let the mesh (fineness) of the partition $P$ be denoted by:
$$
\begin{gather}
\lambda(P) := \max_{1 \le i \le n} \Delta x_i \tag{5}
\end{gather}
$$

# Step 1
Fix an arbitrary index $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$. 

Consider the restriction of $\Phi$ to the subinterval $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$. By hypothesis 1, $\Phi$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, and therefore its restriction is continuous on $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$. By hypothesis 2, $\Phi$ is differentiable on $(a, b)$. Because $(x_{i-1}, x_i) \subseteq (a, b)$, $\Phi$ is differentiable on $(x_{i-1}, x_i)$.

Consequently, the hypotheses of Lagrange's Mean-Value Theorem (Module 5) are fully satisfied by $\Phi$ on the subinterval $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$.

By Lagrange's Mean-Value Theorem, there exists an intermediate point $\xi_i \in (x_{i-1}, x_i)$ such that:
$$
\begin{gather}
\Phi(x_i) - \Phi(x_{i-1}) = \Phi'(\xi_i)(x_i - x_{i-1}) \tag{6}
\end{gather}
$$

Because $\xi_i \in (x_{i-1}, x_i) \subseteq (a, b)$, invoking the differential identity of step 1 at the evaluation node $\xi_i$ gives:
$$
\begin{gather}
\Phi'(\xi_i) = f(\xi_i) \tag{7}
\end{gather}
$$

Substituting step 4 and step 7 into step 6 yields:
$$
\begin{gather}
\Phi(x_i) - \Phi(x_{i-1}) = f(\xi_i)\Delta x_i \tag{8}
\end{gather}
$$

---
# Step 2
Summing the identity of step 8 over all indices $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$:
$$
\begin{gather}
\sum_{i=1}^n \big( \Phi(x_i) - \Phi(x_{i-1}) \big) = \sum_{i=1}^n f(\xi_i)\Delta x_i \tag{9}
\end{gather}
$$

The left-hand side of step 9 expands into a telescoping sum:
$$
\begin{gather}
\sum_{i=1}^n \big( \Phi(x_i) - \Phi(x_{i-1}) \big) = \big( \Phi(x_1) - \Phi(x_0) \big) + \big( \Phi(x_2) - \Phi(x_1) \big) + \dots + \big( \Phi(x_n) - \Phi(x_{n-1}) \big) \tag{10}
\end{gather}
$$

In the expanded summation of step 10, every intermediate evaluation $\Phi(x_i)$ for $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, n-1\}$ appears with both a positive sign and a negative sign, leaving only the boundary terms:
$$
\begin{gather}
\sum_{i=1}^n \big( \Phi(x_i) - \Phi(x_{i-1}) \big) = \Phi(x_n) - \Phi(x_0) \tag{11}
\end{gather}
$$

Substituting the boundary endpoints $x_0 = a$ and $x_n = b$ from step 3 into step 11:
$$
\begin{gather}
\sum_{i=1}^n \big( \Phi(x_i) - \Phi(x_{i-1}) \big) = \Phi(b) - \Phi(a) \tag{12}
\end{gather}
$$

Now examine the right-hand side of step 9. Let $\xi = (\xi_1, \xi_2, \dots, \xi_n)$ denote the vector of intermediate points generated by step 8. The tagged partition $(P, \xi)$ defines the Riemann sum:
$$
\begin{gather}
\sigma(f; P, \xi) := \sum_{i=1}^n f(\xi_i)\Delta x_i \tag{13}
\end{gather}
$$

Equating step 12 and step 13 via step 9 yields the exact algebraic identity:
$$
\begin{gather}
\Phi(b) - \Phi(a) = \sigma(f; P, \xi) \tag{14}
\end{gather}
$$

The quantity $\Phi(b) - \Phi(a)$ is a fixed constant determined exclusively by the primitive $\Phi$ and the boundary points $a$ and $b$; it does not depend on the partition $P$ or the fineness $\lambda(P)$.

---
# Step 3
By hypothesis, $f \in \mathcal{R}[a, b]$. 

By the definition of the Riemann integral (Module 6, Chapter 1), there exists a unique real number $I = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a fineness threshold $\delta > 0$ such that for every partition $P$ of $[a, b]$ satisfying:
$$
\begin{gather}
\lambda(P) < \delta \tag{15}
\end{gather}
$$
and for *any* choice of intermediate sample nodes $\zeta = (\zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_n)$ with $\zeta_i \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]$, the corresponding Riemann sum satisfies:
$$
\begin{gather}
|\sigma(f; P, \zeta) - I| < \varepsilon \tag{16}
\end{gather}
$$

Choose a partition $P$ satisfying the condition $\lambda(P) < \delta$ in step 15.

Because the nodes $\xi_i$ generated by Lagrange's Mean-Value Theorem satisfy $\xi_i \in (x_{i-1}, x_i) \subset [x_{i-1}, x_i]$, the vector $\xi = (\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n)$ is an admissible vector of intermediate sample points for $P$.

Therefore, setting $\zeta = \xi$ in step 16 yields:
$$
\begin{gather}
|\sigma(f; P, \xi) - I| < \varepsilon \tag{17}
\end{gather}
$$

Substituting the identity $\sigma(f; P, \xi) = \Phi(b) - \Phi(a)$ from step 14 into step 17 gives:
$$
\begin{gather}
\left| \big( \Phi(b) - \Phi(a) \big) - I \right| < \varepsilon \tag{18}
\end{gather}
$$

The real number $\big( \Phi(b) - \Phi(a) \big) - I$ is completely independent of the choice of $\varepsilon$. Because the inequality of step 18 holds for every arbitrary strictly positive real number $\varepsilon > 0$, the fundamental property of real numbers ensures that:
$$
\begin{gather}
\big( \Phi(b) - \Phi(a) \big) - I = 0 \tag{19}
\end{gather}
$$

Substituting $I = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ into step 19 yields:
$$
\begin{gather}
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \Phi(b) - \Phi(a) \tag{20}
\end{gather}
$$
which establishes the Newton-Leibniz formula.
$$
\begin{gather}
\textbf{Q.E.D.}
\end{gather}
$$
