Let $X$ be a non-empty set.

---
# Theorem 1

Let $\mathcal{R}$ be an arbitrary equivalence relation on $X$. For any element $x \in X$, the equivalence class generated by $x$ is defined as $[x] := \{y \in X \mid y \mathcal{R} x\}$. To establish that the quotient collection of equivalence classes $X / \mathcal{R} := \{[x] \mid x \in X\}$ forms a valid partition of $X$, the three defining conditions of a partition must be satisfied:

**1. Non-emptiness:**
By the reflexivity of the equivalence relation $\mathcal{R}$, every element $x \in X$ satisfies $x \mathcal{R} x$. Consequently:
$$
\begin{gather}
x \in [x] \tag{1}
\end{gather}
$$
Because every equivalence class $[x]$ contains at least its generator $x$, it follows that:
$$
\begin{gather}
\forall [x] \in X / \mathcal{R} \quad ([x] \neq \emptyset) \tag{2}
\end{gather}
$$

**2. Pairwise Disjointness:**
Let $[x], [y] \in X / \mathcal{R}$ such that $[x] \neq [y]$. To prove that $[x] \cap [y] = \emptyset$, proceed by contradiction. Assume that:
$$
\begin{gather}
[x] \cap [y] \neq \emptyset \tag{3}
\end{gather}
$$
Step 3 guarantees the existence of at least one witness element $z \in [x] \cap [y]$. By the definition of an equivalence class:
$$
\begin{gather}
z \mathcal{R} x \quad \wedge \quad z \mathcal{R} y \tag{4}
\end{gather}
$$
Applying the symmetry of $\mathcal{R}$ to $z \mathcal{R} x$ gives $x \mathcal{R} z$. Combining $x \mathcal{R} z$ and $z \mathcal{R} y$ via the transitivity of $\mathcal{R}$ yields:
$$
\begin{gather}
x \mathcal{R} y \tag{5}
\end{gather}
$$
Now let $w \in [x]$ be an arbitrary element. By definition, $w \mathcal{R} x$. Applying transitivity to $w \mathcal{R} x$ and $x \mathcal{R} y$ from step 5 yields:
$$
\begin{gather}
w \mathcal{R} x \wedge x \mathcal{R} y \implies w \mathcal{R} y \implies w \in [y] \tag{6}
\end{gather}
$$
Because $w \in [x]$ was arbitrary, step 6 establishes that $[x] \subseteq [y]$. By symmetry of argument, reversing the roles of $x$ and $y$ confirms that $[y] \subseteq [x]$. By the Principle of Extensionality:
$$
\begin{gather}
[x] = [y] \tag{7}
\end{gather}
$$
Step 7 directly contradicts the initial premise that $[x] \neq [y]$. Consequently, the hypothesis in step 3 must be rejected, establishing that:
$$
\begin{gather}
[x] \neq [y] \implies [x] \cap [y] = \emptyset \tag{8}
\end{gather}
$$

**3. Collective Exhaustiveness:**
To verify that the union of all equivalence classes recovers $X$, evaluate mutual subset inclusion:
$$
\begin{gather}
\bigcup_{[x] \in X / \mathcal{R}} [x] = X \tag{9}
\end{gather}
$$
By definition, each equivalence class $[x]$ is a subset of $X$, which guarantees the forward inclusion $\bigcup_{[x] \in X / \mathcal{R}} [x] \subseteq X$. Conversely, let $x \in X$ be an arbitrary element. By reflexivity (step 1), $x \in [x]$. Because $[x]$ is a member of the family $X / \mathcal{R}$, it follows that:
$$
\begin{gather}
x \in \bigcup_{[z] \in X / \mathcal{R}} [z] \tag{10}
\end{gather}
$$
Because $x \in X$ was arbitrary, step 10 establishes that $X \subseteq \bigcup_{[x] \in X / \mathcal{R}} [x]$. Mutual inclusion confirms step 9.

Combining step 2, step 8, and step 9 confirms that $X / \mathcal{R}$ forms a valid partition of $X$.

# Theorem 2

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be an arbitrary partition of $X$. Define the binary relation $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}$ on $X$ by:
$$
\begin{gather}
x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} y \iff \exists A \in \mathcal{P} \text{ such that } (x \in A \wedge y \in A) \tag{11}
\end{gather}
$$

**Verification of Equivalence Relation Axioms:**
1. **Reflexivity:** Let $x \in X$. By the collective exhaustiveness of the partition $\mathcal{P}$, there exists at least one block $A \in \mathcal{P}$ such that $x \in A$. Consequently, $x \in A \wedge x \in A$, which establishes:
$$
\begin{gather}
\forall x \in X \quad (x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} x) \tag{12}
\end{gather}
$$
2. **Symmetry:** Let $x, y \in X$ such that $x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} y$. By step 11, there exists $A \in \mathcal{P}$ such that $x \in A \wedge y \in A$. By the commutativity of logical conjunction, $y \in A \wedge x \in A$, which confirms:
$$
\begin{gather}
\forall x, y \in X \quad (x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} y \implies y \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} x) \tag{13}
\end{gather}
$$
3. **Transitivity:** Let $x, y, z \in X$ such that $x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} y$ and $y \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} z$. By step 11, there exist blocks $A, B \in \mathcal{P}$ such that:
$$
\begin{gather}
(x \in A \wedge y \in A) \quad \wedge \quad (y \in B \wedge z \in B) \tag{14}
\end{gather}
$$
Because $y \in A$ and $y \in B$, it follows that $y \in A \cap B$, meaning $A \cap B \neq \emptyset$. By the pairwise disjointness condition of the partition $\mathcal{P}$, two blocks with non-empty intersection must be identical:
$$
\begin{gather}
A = B \tag{15}
\end{gather}
$$
Substituting $B = A$ into step 14 yields $x \in A \wedge z \in A$. Because $A \in \mathcal{P}$, step 11 implies:
$$
\begin{gather}
x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} z \tag{16}
\end{gather}
$$
Combining step 12, step 13, and step 16 establishes that $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}$ is a valid equivalence relation on $X$.

**Uniqueness and Partition Recovery:**
For any element $x \in X$, let $A_x \in \mathcal{P}$ denote the unique partition block containing $x$. The equivalence class generated by $x$ under $\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}$ satisfies:
$$
\begin{gather}
[x]_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}} = \{y \in X \mid y \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} x\} = \{y \in X \mid \exists A \in \mathcal{P} \, (y \in A \wedge x \in A)\} \tag{17}
\end{gather}
$$
Because $A_x$ is the unique block in $\mathcal{P}$ containing $x$, any block $A \in \mathcal{P}$ with $x \in A$ must satisfy $A = A_x$. Consequently, step 17 simplifies to:
$$
\begin{gather}
[x]_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}} = \{y \in X \mid y \in A_x\} = A_x \tag{18}
\end{gather}
$$
Taking the collection of all such equivalence classes recovers the original partition:
$$
\begin{gather}
X / \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} = \{[x]_{\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}} \mid x \in X\} = \{A_x \mid x \in X\} = \mathcal{P} \tag{19}
\end{gather}
$$
Finally, let $\mathcal{R}'$ be an arbitrary equivalence relation on $X$ whose quotient set satisfies $X / \mathcal{R}' = \mathcal{P}$. Then for any $x, y \in X$:
$$
\begin{gather}
x \mathcal{R}' y \iff [x]_{\mathcal{R}'} = [y]_{\mathcal{R}'} \iff \exists A \in \mathcal{P} \, (x \in A \wedge y \in A) \iff x \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}} y \tag{20}
\end{gather}
$$
Step 20 confirms that $\mathcal{R}' = \mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}$, establishing the uniqueness of the induced relation.
$$
\begin{gather}
\textbf{Q.E.D.}
\end{gather}
